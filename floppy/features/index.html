The Floppy feature guide

What does Floppy do?

Floppy is a self-hosted, all-in-one media tracker and personal media diary for everything you watch, read, play, or listen to. It gives your whole media life one library, one history, and more useful ways to decide what comes next.

Try the live demo See the overview
✦
Think of it as your media life, with a memory.

Floppy combines a watchlist, progress tracker, media diary, recap dashboard, owned-media shelf, and discovery tool in one place you can run yourself.

✦

Track more than one kind of media.

Movies and TV do not have to live in one app, books in another, and music somewhere else. Floppy treats each kind of media as useful in its own way, while keeping them together in the same personal library.

▶

Make time easier to use

Know what is next, and why.

A tracker should help after you save something. Floppy turns a large collection into a clearer daily starting point, whether you have ten minutes for an episode or a weekend for a long game.

Progress

See time left, not just a status.

Episode counts, reading progress, runtime, and game-length data make “what can I finish?” easier to answer.

Home

Shape the first screen around you.

Choose the rows that matter, reorder them, and surface library queries, custom lists, smart lists, or things you started but have not rated.

Next up

Find the next good thing faster.

Progress views, upcoming releases, recommendations, and a livelier home reduce the distance between “I want to watch something” and a real choice.

↻

Remember what you have done

Make your history useful again.

Your history is more than a list of dates. It can show the shape of your year, help you find an old favorite, and give you a better sense of what your attention has actually gone to.

One history

Scan everything in one feed.

Watches, listens, reading activity, and other media history can sit together, with filters for media type, genre, and time.

Useful when: you remember the feeling of something, but not which app it was in.

Statistics

Look back with context.

Refreshable recaps, custom date ranges, compare mode, top talent, listening time, pages read, and reading streaks make stats worth revisiting.

Useful when: you want a year-in-review that includes more than movies.

People and places

Follow the connections.

Move from a title to its actors, directors, authors, studios, filmographies, discographies, and related works instead of stopping at one detail page.

Useful when: one great thing makes you curious about everything around it.

☷

Organize the collection

Turn “save for later” into a system.

Lists and collections become more powerful when they keep up with your life. Floppy gives you simple ways to sort what you own, what you have started, and what you want to share.

Owned media

Keep track of what you actually own.

Collections can describe the copy on your shelf or drive, including source, resolution, HDR, format, codec, and bitrate, without losing the watch history.

Example: find the 4K copy you own that you still have not watched.

Smart lists

Ask your library a question.

Build lists from collection status, release state, platform, origin, author, tags, rating, progress, and more. The list updates as your library changes.

Example: unfinished books by authors you rate highly.

Shareable lists

Share a point of view, not a spreadsheet.

Public and private lists, custom slugs, profile pages, completion percentages, RSS, JSON feeds, and approval-based recommendations give lists a life beyond your own account.

Example: publish a watchlist friends can follow as it changes.

⇄

Bring your media stack together

Connect the services you already use.

Floppy can be the place where your services meet. Integrations are designed around useful workflows, so your library can stay current without turning every update into a manual import.

From playback to listening to library automation: connect the tools that already hold pieces of your media life, then keep the history and context together in Floppy.

Media servers

Plex, Jellyfin, and Emby

Bring in libraries, watchlists, ratings, playback events, and now-playing context through the media servers you already run.

Listening

Pocket Casts, Last.fm, and Audiobookshelf

Keep podcast accounts, listening history, scrobbles, and audiobook activity moving into the same history as everything else.

Automation

Radarr, Sonarr, and Seerr

Use scheduled library syncs and Seerr webhooks so wanted media can move from request to collection with less repetition.

Imports

Bring over the history you already have.

Import from Trakt, Simkl, MyAnimeList, AniList, Kitsu, Steam, Goodreads, StoryGraph, Hardcover, IMDb, HowLongToBeat, Grouvee, and more.

✧

Make it fit your life

Useful defaults, room to make it yours.

A personal media tracker should not make every person use it the same way. Floppy gives you control over what you see, how you sort it, and how you connect to it.

Your screens

Choose what deserves attention.

  • Configure home rows and their order.
  • Reorder columns for each table and list view.
  • Choose duration, rating, language, title, subtitle, and mobile display preferences.

Your account

Keep access practical and secure.

  • Give everyone in your home their own account and preferences.
  • Use simple sign-in and recovery options when you need them.
  • Choose how long you stay signed in, and turn notifications on only when they help.

Your tools

Build around the library.

  • Connect Floppy to the tools you already use.
  • For advanced setups, use its API and MCP server.
  • Keep imports, exports, and automations close to one source of truth.

Your data

Take it with you.

  • Export a portable copy whenever you need one.
  • Schedule regular backups and exports.
  • Keep your library on hardware you control and decide where it lives.

Self-host it with confidence

Your media history should belong to you.

Floppy runs in Docker and keeps its database on hardware you control. The point is not to make setup feel like a hobby project. It is to give a long-term library a home that is portable, inspectable, and yours.

Start simply. Run the app and Redis together with Docker Compose or Portainer.
Stay resilient. Use backups, scheduled exports, recovery tools, and safer handling for a library that keeps growing.
Keep your options open. CSV import and export means your history is not trapped in one service.
